Why pain complexity matters

Pain complexity helps make the recovery picture clearer.

Pain intensity asks:

"How much does it hurt?"

A useful signal — but it may change from day to day, and it does not explain what may be shaping recovery or what the plan should prioritize.

Pain complexity asks:

"What may be interfering with recovery?"

Flares, sleep, function, treatment burden, healthcare experiences, confidence, medical history, support needs, and readiness to engage.

Complexity level guides care-planning intensity — how much support, structure, and collaboration a plan may need. It is not a risk score and not a diagnosis.

One case. Many interacting layers.
Pain & symptom patternFlare instabilityFunctionSleepEmotional loadCognitive interferenceMedical comorbiditiesComplications from proceduresTreatment historyFear & confidenceHealthcare experiencesSupport & life demands

Which layers may be shaping recovery? A complexity-informed assessment can help make them visible.

Complexity-informed care starts with asking better questions.

Why this matters to healthcare organizations

From assessment findings to defensible, coordinated care.

Better documentation

Records more clearly demonstrate complexity, medical necessity, clinical action, and response to care.

More defensible care plans

Plans reflect function, risk, barriers, goals, monitoring, and coordination — not only pain intensity.

Clearer team communication

Teams share a common understanding of what has been assessed, what has changed, and what is needed next.

Improved patient experience

Patients receive a coherent explanation of their recovery needs instead of fragmented recommendations.

Stronger documentation may support cleaner claims, clearer medical necessity, better audit preparedness, and more consistent care-management workflows.

About the educator

Marisela Cigliuti, BSN, RN, CCDS

She is a registered nurse, pain recovery educator, and founder of Pain Care At Home™. Her professional background includes nursing, neurology, case management, clinical documentation, quality improvement, complex-care review, and interdisciplinary coordination.

Her pain education work is also informed by training in Pain Reprocessing Therapy, Emotional Awareness and Expression Therapy, mindfulness, trauma-informed care, and her own lived experience with persistent pain.

The education is informed by the Pain Complexity Screening Tool™ framework — a structured approach for identifying recovery barriers that may affect function, engagement, treatment matching, and care planning. It is a care-planning framework, not a diagnostic instrument, and it never replaces clinical judgment.
