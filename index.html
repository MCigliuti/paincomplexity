When Chronic Pain Recovery Stalls,
Look Beyond the Pain Score.

The Pain Complexity Screening Tool™ (PCST) is a pilot-stage, nurse-developed care-planning framework that helps organize patient-reported recovery barriers and supports more individualized conversations about recovery.

By Marisela Cigliuti, BSN, RN, CCDS — registered nurse & clinical documentation specialist.

For clinicians and care teams

Does this sound familiar?

You have patients who —

  • Continue to struggle despite appropriate treatment
  • Have seen multiple providers without lasting improvement
  • Feel overwhelmed, discouraged, or unsure what to try next
  • Have recovery barriers that don’t fit neatly into a diagnosis or pain score

The challenge may not be identifying the pain. It may be understanding what’s making recovery harder.

The real question

Pain intensity asks one question. Recovery asks another.

Chronic pain assessment often answers:

  • Where does it hurt?
  • How intense is the pain?
  • What is the diagnosis?

A useful starting point — but it may not explain what’s shaping recovery or what the plan should prioritize.

But when recovery stalls, the more useful questions are often about:

  • Flare patterns
  • Function
  • Confidence
  • Healthcare experiences
  • Daily life
  • Recovery readiness

Recovery isn’t shaped by pain alone.

About the framework

The Pain Complexity Screening Tool™

The PCST is a pilot-stage framework designed to help organize the factors that may be making recovery more difficult into meaningful domains — supporting clearer clinical conversations and more individualized care planning.

It is intended to support, not replace, clinical judgment.

The goal

Why This Matters

Understanding pain is important. Understanding why recovery has stalled may be even more important.

The goal isn’t to create another score. The goal is to support better conversations, clearer care planning, and more individualized recovery strategies.

About the educator

Marisela Cigliuti, BSN, RN, CCDS

She is a registered nurse, pain recovery educator, and founder of Pain Care At Home™, with a background in neurology, case management, clinical documentation, and complex-care review.

Her pain education work is also informed by training in Pain Reprocessing Therapy, Emotional Awareness and Expression Therapy, mindfulness, trauma-informed care, and her own lived experience with persistent pain.

The education is informed by the Pain Complexity Screening Tool™ framework — a structured approach for identifying recovery barriers that may affect function, engagement, treatment matching, and care planning. It is a care-planning framework, not a diagnostic instrument, and it never replaces clinical judgment.
