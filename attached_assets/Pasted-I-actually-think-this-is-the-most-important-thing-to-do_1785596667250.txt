I actually think this is the most important thing to do **before** you spend money on LinkedIn ads.

Right now, paincomplexity.com is still describing **what the PCST is**. The website needs to answer **why a clinician should care**.

Your audience isn't searching for a screening tool. They're struggling with patients who aren't improving.

## I would completely reposition the homepage.

### Hero Section

**Headline**

# When Chronic Pain Recovery Stalls, Look Beyond the Pain Score.

**Subheadline**

The Pain Complexity Screening Tool™ (PCST) is a pilot-stage, nurse-developed care-planning framework that helps clinicians organize patient-reported recovery barriers and support more individualized conversations about recovery.

**Button**

**Schedule a Free Provider Consultation**

### Then immediately answer the clinician's question

## Does this sound familiar?

You have patients who...

* Continue to struggle despite appropriate treatment.
* Have seen multiple providers without lasting improvement.
* Feel overwhelmed, discouraged, or unsure what to try next.
* Have recovery barriers that don't fit neatly into a diagnosis or pain score.

The challenge may not be identifying the pain.

It may be understanding what is making recovery harder.

---

## Introduce the problem

### Chronic pain assessment often answers:

* Where does it hurt?
* How intense is the pain?
* What is the diagnosis?

But when recovery stalls, clinicians often need different questions.

Questions about:

* flare patterns
* function
* confidence
* healthcare experiences
* daily life
* recovery readiness

Because recovery isn't shaped by pain alone.

---

## Then introduce the PCST

# The Pain Complexity Screening Tool™

The PCST is a pilot-stage framework designed to help organize patient-reported recovery barriers into meaningful domains that support clinical conversations and individualized care planning.

It is intended to support—not replace—clinical judgment.

---

## Then explain why it matters

# Why This Matters

Understanding pain is important.

Understanding why recovery has stalled may be even more important.

The goal isn't to create another score.

The goal is to support better conversations, clearer care planning, and more individualized recovery strategies.

# One thing I would remove completely

I would remove language like:

> "Pain Complexity Screening Tool"

as the first thing people see.

Instead, lead with the problem:

> **When chronic pain recovery stalls...**

That's what clinicians experience every day.

The PCST becomes the solution—not the headline.

## One more strategic recommendation

I also think the site should shift from talking about **pain complexity** to talking more about **recovery complexity**.

That's where your work is becoming distinctive.

Instead of saying:

> "The Pain Complexity Screening Tool assesses pain complexity."

Say:

> **The Pain Complexity Screening Tool helps organize the factors that may be making recovery more difficult.**

That subtle shift aligns with everything you've been building:

* your LinkedIn content,
* the workshops,
* the patient pathway,
* and the evolving vision of the PCST as a care-planning system rather than another pain assessment tool.

If someone leaves your website remembering only one idea, it should be this:

> **When chronic pain recovery stalls, the next step may not be another treatment—it may be a better understanding of what's getting in the way of recovery.**
